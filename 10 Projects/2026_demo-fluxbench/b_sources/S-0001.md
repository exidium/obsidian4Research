## Summary
- Hardware design engineer building a 48 V to 12 V, 6 A buck stage for a motor controller; first-time FluxBench user, forced in by part-family models. Ripple disagreed with a long-used spreadsheet by about thirty percent because the switching frequency was silently reset to 400 kHz on part change; the vendor forum calls this "by design". Trust is granted per result: thermal matched the prototype (68 vs 71 degC on the FET), so only thermal is trusted; ripple still unverified against the scope.
- Sign-off happens on PDF exports pasted into a review document, not on the design file; exports carry no revision stamp, a reviewer has no viewer, and the one live demo of the eleven-minute efficiency sweep froze the UI in front of six people. Four revisions so far, each about an hour of re-export and BOM rework.
- Everything leaves the tool as a picture: schematic retyped into CAD (netlist format unsupported, one divider swap caught in review), layout guidance and firmware timing handed over as screenshots, BOM CSV swapped to approved-vendor parts via a home-made lookup table (about seventy percent coverage).
- Lab network blocks activation, help, forum and the thirty-day license check-in; node-locked seat cannot move to the lab machine. Support replied politely but from a template and offline activation is enterprise-only. Vendor documentation is organised by screen; an internal "FluxBench gotchas" wiki page (eight items, written by two users) is the real manual. Compared with a competitor trial (Ampergy Workbench), FluxBench wins on part picker and thermal, loses on transparency.
- Value seen as fair at roughly two and a half thousand per seat, but trust gaps mean they would currently rather have one competitor seat than two FluxBench seats; fixing silent changes, revision stamps and reviewer access would lead them to argue for four seats. Workarounds: seven-item advanced-panel checklist, sweep resolution cut from forty to twelve points (eleven minutes to about three and a half). Magic wand: the tool should tell them what it changed and why.
