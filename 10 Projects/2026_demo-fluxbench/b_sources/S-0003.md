## Summary
- Validation engineer at a medical device maker; receives simulated designs, proves them on the eval kit and then on their own board, and every out-of-limit discrepancy becomes a numbered investigation signed by a second engineer.
- Measured 89 percent efficiency against a simulated 92 and ripple 1.4 times the simulated value; an eval-kit capacitor revision explained the ripple gap after six hours of work, while the efficiency gap stays unresolved because loss is shown as one number.
- Report workflow: bench data arrives by script, simulation numbers are read off a cursor and retyped (about fifty per report, a day of work, errors caught only by the reviewer); wants CSV export with the run's assumptions in the header and a scripting interface.
- Temperature sweeps crash about one in three with no log and no place to report it; project files are forked with dates and initials, model updates change old results silently, so old installers are archived to reproduce numbers for audits.
- Trust is earned per result and lives in a self-maintained checklist and known-gaps sheet; quality would accept a vendor accuracy statement (typical and max deviation, conditions, basis, revision) in place of personal correlation runs, and competitor tools (RailForge, Tessaline) export data but lack the models.
